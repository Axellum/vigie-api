# Tides: how far is the computed tide from the real one?

*A measure made on 28 October 2026 by [`tools/measure_tides.py`](../../tools/measure_tides.py), which applies the criterion of
[ADR 0015](../decisions/0015-tides-from-a-global-model.md) as it was written before the measure (the first commit of the pull request that added it). The figures of the tables
are printed by the tool; its results are in [`tides-2026-10-results.json.gz`](tides-2026-10-results.json.gz). The model is what
`GET /v1/tides` serves: EOT20, the nearest cell within 30 km, the code of `app/services/tides.py`.*

## 1. Conclusion

- **France: the criterion holds.** 9 of the 10 ports pass. From Calais to Saint-Jean-de-Luz the high and low waters come within 3 to 18
  minutes (median) of the observed ones, their heights within 5 to 25 cm (2 to 5 % of the range), and the whole curve within 7 to 35 cm
  RMS (3 to 6 %). **Le Havre fails, narrowly**: 23 minutes on the times (20 allowed); its long high water stand (the shallow-water tide of
  the Seine bay) is not well drawn by a model that has M4 but not MS4, MN4 or M6.
- **United States: the criterion fails.** 5 of the 10 stations pass (Boston, Bob Hall Pier, San Diego, San Francisco, Honolulu). The five
  that fail are in a harbour, a bay or a sound that a cell of 1/8 degree does not enter: the model gives the tide **of the open coast
  nearby, which comes earlier** (The Battery, New York: 67 minutes early; Charleston: 21; Virginia Key, in Biscayne Bay: 43; Pensacola,
  inside its bay: 2 h 22 and 22 of its 54 extremes unmatched). **Seattle is wrong**: in Puget Sound EOT20's M2 has 0.91 m and a phase
  124 degrees from NOAA's harmonic constant (1.063 m), about four hours; no extreme matches.
- **Outside the criterion**, as chosen beforehand: Bayonne Boucau would pass; Arcachon (inside the lagoon) has its tide an hour later
  than the model (57 minutes); Marseille has a tide of 16 cm, which the model gets within 19 minutes but not within 10 % of its range
  (the weather moves the sea more than the tide does there); Anchorage, at the head of Cook Inlet, is 24 minutes late on the model with a
  height within 3.5 %.

**Reading.** On the open coasts the tide of EOT20 is good enough for a beach, a fishing trip on foot or a surf session: within a quarter of
an hour and a few percent of the range, Atlantic and Channel of France included. **Inside a bay, a harbour, an estuary, a lagoon or a sound
it is not**: the real tide comes later, by up to an hour or two in the cases measured, and in Puget Sound the model is simply wrong. The
answer of `GET /v1/tides` says so in every answer; it does not know whether a given point is inside a bay. A model with a finer grid
(FES2022, 1/30 degree) is the next step for those places (section 5).

## 2. Method

- **Window**: 2026-09-27 00:00 to 2026-10-27 00:00 UTC, 30 days (two spring-neap cycles, with the equinoctial spring tides).
- **France**: REFMAR raw observations, one value a minute, smoothed by a centred 61-minute mean (a minute counts when at least 31 of the 61
  exist), read every 10 minutes; their high and low waters are the extremes of the smoothed one-minute curve. They contain the weather
  (surge, seiches), which the model does not.
- **United States**: NOAA's predictions on the mean sea level, every 10 minutes, and its high and low waters (`hilo`): a tide without
  weather, from NOAA's own harmonic constants of each station.
- **Model**: the nearest EOT20 cell kept by the API (the distance is in the tables), every 10 minutes, and its extremes from a one-minute
  curve. A dip of less than 2 cm between two extremes is dropped (the same rule as the API).
- **Measures and thresholds**: those of the ADR. The series are taken relative to their own mean. The *mean range* is the mean difference
  between consecutive high and low waters of the reference; a *significant* reference extreme differs by at least 25 % of the mean range
  from both neighbours; it is matched with the model extreme of the same kind nearest in time, within 3 hours.

## 5. The estimated coefficient (outside the criterion)

- **Against the same formula on the observed tide of Brest**, over the window: 58 high waters, all matched; the estimate is 3 points from
  the observed value (median), 2.1 lower on average, 10 at most. This measures the model at Brest, not SHOM's official coefficient, which
  is not used.
- **Over 2026 to 2044** (13,412 high waters at Brest, computed every 5 minutes) the estimate goes from **25 to 118**: close to the 20 to
  120 that the coefficient scale is said to span (secondary sources; SHOM's own text could not be read).

## 6. Limits, and what comes next

- **One month, one season.** The autumn brings surges that the observations of France contain; they weigh on the heights and the RMS, a
  little on the times.
- **NOAA's predictions are a model too**, from long series at the station itself; they are the best reference there is for the tide alone.
- **The extremes of a smoothed curve** are moved slightly by the smoothing where the tide is asymmetric (a few minutes at most for a
  61-minute mean); the model's are not smoothed.
- **Where the API cannot know**: whether a point is inside a bay. The answer warns about it in words; it does not flag one place or
  another.
- **FES2022** (CNES, LEGOS, NOVELTIS, CLS, distributed by AVISO; 1/30 degree, 34 constituents with MS4, MN4, M6...) should follow
  harbours and bays better. It needs a free registration that is the owner's to make: the
  [registration form](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/en/data/data-access/registration-form.html) of AVISO+, product "FES (Finite Element
  Solution - Oceanic Tides Heights)", accepting the AVISO licence. Its files are about 170 MB per constituent: the coastal band would be
  measured and sized before it replaces anything.
