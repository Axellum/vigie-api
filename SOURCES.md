# Sources, licences and credits

The **code** of this repository is under the [MIT licence](LICENSE). The **data** it relays belongs to
each provider and stays under that provider's own terms: this page lists them, one row per source.

The Vigie API is a personal, **non-commercial** project, made with the help of AI and shared in case it
helps. It is **not an official warning service**: the alerts are relayed from public feeds, can be late,
incomplete or wrong, and must not be the only thing a decision rests on. **GDACS alerts are automatic and
do not replace official warnings.**

How to read this page:

- Checked on **2026-10-20** by opening the pages linked below (some through an automated reader that
  summarizes a page: re-read the wording that matters in a browser before any public launch). What could
  not be checked is marked **not verified**. Terms change: re-check them before any change of use
  (sharing the data, a public instance, anything commercial).
- **Credit in the responses** is the text the API puts in the `attribution` field of the block of data
  from that source. The texts are declared once, in [`app/attribution.py`](app/attribution.py), and a test
  checks that this page quotes them exactly. *Wording*: **provider** = the provider gives this text;
  **suggested** = the provider names a credit line and this is it; **ours** = the provider prescribes no
  wording, the text is ours (it names the source and, where the terms ask for it, says that the alert is
  automatic or that the provider does not endorse this service).
- **Quota** is what the provider publishes. Where one is known the API holds itself to 90 % of it
  (`VIGIE_BUDGET_FRACTION`, declared in `app/collectors/budget.py`); where none is published the calls are
  counted but not limited. How the data is cached and how many calls it costs: [`docs/data-sources.md`](docs/data-sources.md).

## Official weather warnings (vigilances)

`GET /v1/vigilances` relays the warnings that the national weather services publish, with their words
untouched. It adds three fields of its own (`status`, `level`, `phenomenon`), listed in `derived` on every
warning, and keeps the source's wording next to them (`level_original`, `phenomenon_original`, `event`,
`texts`). Nothing is translated, shortened or rewritten. The tables that derive the three fields are in
[`docs/data-sources.md`](docs/data-sources.md).

Which source answers for a country (the rule, and the reasons, in [ADR 0003](docs/decisions/0003-national-source-preferred-rivers-are-lines.md)):

A river section is a **line**, not an area: a position is matched when it lies within `river_radius_km` of it (5 km by default, 50 at most), and the distance is returned with the warning (`distance_km`).

**Duplicates between MeteoAlarm and Vigicrues.** For France the same flood can be said twice: as a colour for the département (MeteoAlarm, type "Flooding", Météo-France's "inondation") and as a colour for a section of river (Vigicrues). ## Countries with no source (and why)

The borders (`app/data/countries.geojson.gz`, Natural Earth, public domain: the table "Shapes" above) hold **every country**, so a
position finds its country whether or not the instance reads a source for it. A position in a country with no source is answered with
the country and the reason (`uncovered` and `note` of `GET /v1/vigilances`; `GET /v1/vigilances/countries?uncovered=true` lists all of
them): a stable `reason_code`, for a client to translate, and one English sentence. A territory has its own ISO code (Puerto Rico `PR`,
Greenland `GL`, the overseas departments of France `GF` `GP` `MQ` `RE` `YT`...) and is answered on its own.

**A reason is written only when it was read.** A country nothing is known about is `no_source`: "No official warning feed is read by
this instance for this country." It says neither that the country has no warning service nor that its terms forbid anything.

| Code | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `no_source` | No official warning feed is read for the country, and nothing more is known. |
| `licence_unverified` | A national service exists (the WMO registry lists it, or the research of 2026-10-20 names it), but its reuse terms were not verified, or none were found. A service of the registry is then read only with `VIGIE_WMO_SOURCES=all`. |
| `terms_restrict` | The terms found for the service ask for its permission before its warnings are passed on (Indonesia, Vanuatu, South Africa: the rows of their services above). |
| `terms_contradictory` | The terms found contradict each other, for example a feed that says "public domain" and a site that restricts copying (Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Singapore, Thailand, Trinidad and Tobago: the rows of their services above; Hong Kong, below). |

| `source_off` | A source exists but this instance turned it off: MetService (`VIGIE_ENABLE_METSERVICE=0`), or the whole registry (`VIGIE_WMO_SOURCES=off`). |

## Where the credits go

- **JSON responses.** `attribution` on each hazard alert (`/v1/snapshot`, `/v1/point`, `/v1/history`,
  `/v1/geojson/hazards`, `/v1/cap/alerts`, `/v1/stream`; a hazard that names a fault in `near_places` also carries the citation of the GEM
  database where `near_places` is present), on each zone of the snapshot (the weather, plus
  the air quality when its index was added), and on the `rain_nowcast`, `marine`, `flood_risk`,
  `air_quality` and `space_weather` blocks of `/v1/point`. Where one object mixes several blocks the
  field is named after what it credits: `weather_attribution` in `/v1/point`,
  `global_status.space_weather_attribution` in the snapshot. A block with no data has `attribution: null`.
  A simulated alert or block is credited to no provider.
- **GeoJSON and CAP.** The attribution is a property of each feature (and in `metadata.attribution`), a
  parameter of each CAP alert and the last sentence of its description.
- **Official warnings.** `attribution` on each warning (the credit, then the issuing service once its CAP
  document is read; for the DWD, ECCC, MetService and Vigicrues the credit comes with the warning, and the
  Vigicrues one says the date of the last update of the flow), `attribution` and `disclaimer` on the answer of
  `/v1/vigilances` (only the sources that answered are credited) and in `metadata` of
  `/v1/vigilances.geojson`, and on each `vigilance_state` / `vigilance_push` message of `/v1/stream`.
- **Dashboard.** The page ends with the table above (credits and links) and the notice that GDACS
  alerts are automatic and do not replace official warnings.
- **The compact frame** (`/v1/embedded/frame`) has **no** credit field: its contract is fixed
  ([`docs/embedded-frame.md`](docs/embedded-frame.md)). A display that shows its data credits the sources
  itself (the display repository: whether it does so was not checked).

## Before running a public instance

This project was built for one person's home. Each of these points comes from the terms above:

1. Open-Meteo's free API is for non-commercial use, within its quota: a public instance with many clients
   needs a paid plan or a self-hosted Open-Meteo.
2. RainViewer's API is for personal and educational use: keep `VIGIE_ENABLE_RAINVIEWER=0` unless RainViewer agrees in writing.
3. Set `VIGIE_CONTACT` to a real e-mail address or URL: MET Norway and NOAA require an identifying contact.
4. Keep the credits and the "not an official warning" notice wherever the data is shown, and the disclaimer
   of MeteoAlarm wherever its warnings are shown (it is in the `disclaimer` field). Do not rewrite the words of an
   official warning: the DWD wants its source details deleted if a warning is changed, and ECCC wants no alteration
   of the content or intent of an alert; MetService's Code of Practice asks for no modification either.
5. Leave the simulator off (`VIGIE_ENABLE_SIMULATION` unset) and do not expose the unauthenticated routes
   (`POST /v1/zones`, `POST` and `DELETE /v1/watched-points`, `POST /v1/scheduler/trigger`) to the Internet; the watched-point writes
   are also off unless `VIGIE_ENABLE_WATCHED_POINTS_WRITE=1`. Set `VIGIE_ADMIN_TOKEN` as well: every route that changes something then asks for
   `Authorization: Bearer <token>` (behind TLS, since the token is a shared secret in a header).
6. MetService (New Zealand): the feed says CC BY 4.0 and its use is subject to a Code of Practice, but the website terms
   it points to allow personal use only and forbid resupply (see its row above). For one person's home that is no
   problem; before relaying New Zealand's warnings to other people, ask MetService (the CAP page gives an address for
   developers: no e-mail was sent) or keep `VIGIE_ENABLE_METSERVICE=0`. The source is on by default, also in the Docker
   image; off, a position in New Zealand gets no source and the answer says why.
7. The services of the WMO registry: leave `VIGIE_WMO_SOURCES` at `verified`. With `all`, the API relays the warnings of 88 services
   whose terms are unknown, contradictory or restrictive (Singapore, Thailand, Kuwait, Trinidad and Tobago publish terms that
   restrict copying); that is for an instance only its owner uses. Even the verified ones ask for their credit: keep it with the
   warning, as the API puts it in `attribution`.
8. Watched points (`/v1/watched-points`): they are never in the hazards, but the list and the status are readable by anyone who can reach the
   API. Do not watch a home on an instance that others can read. The fault file is CC BY-SA 4.0 (share-alike): keep its
   notice (`app/data/gem-active-faults.NOTICE.md`) with any copy of it, and the citation in the `attribution` of a hazard that names a fault.
